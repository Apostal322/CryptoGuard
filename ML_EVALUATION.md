## Task

Classify an Ethereum transaction as part of a phishing operation or not, from information available when it executed: the transaction, its receipt, and the sender's nonce and target's code at the previous block. Labels come from PTXPhish (NDSS 2025), reviewed by two security experts and independent of CryptoGuard. Many labelled phishing transactions are the *attacker's* (for ice phishing, the `transferFrom` that drains the victim), so this is not a pre-signing warning for a victim's own approval.

## Limitations

- One labelled source. PTXPhish's benign class is transactions of prominent accounts ("KOL") and developers, not a random sample of Ethereum users; precision on real traffic, where phishing is far rarer than here, will be much lower than measured.
- The `no_identity` rows show how much depends on who sends a transaction (long histories, vanity addresses) rather than what it does; those signals partly reflect how the dataset was built.
- Small test split after the time window and account purge; intervals are wide.
- The CryptoGuard comparison is a transaction-level proxy of its signals, not its wallet-centric detectors, which need wallet history this dataset lacks.
- Features use the receipt (logs of the executed transaction). Scoring a transaction before it is signed would need a simulation, which is not implemented.
- Not independently reviewed. The model never creates findings (shadow mode only).
