# Cross-dataset validation of poisoning research data (Phase 11.3)

Status: research only. No model was trained. 0 Ethereum RPC requests. Every number below
comes from [ML_CROSS_DATASET_TABLES.md](ML_CROSS_DATASET_TABLES.md) (generated by
`cryptoguard ml cross-dataset-report`) or [ML_POISONING_TABLES.md](ML_POISONING_TABLES.md)
(`cryptoguard ml poisoning-report`); raw and address-level files stay in the git-ignored
`data/` directory.

## 1. Executive summary

- **The archive is intact and read correctly.** Tsuchiya et al.'s attempt archive (2.32 GB
  gzip, 9.85 GB of SQL) was streamed once without being executed:
  - **34,905,969 rows, 0 malformed**;
  - every one of the publisher's ten reference counts reproduced exactly (17,365,954
    poisoning transfers, 1,691,529 poisoning transactions, 1,330,948 victims, 6,492,215
    lookalikes, ...);
  - peak memory under 290 MiB, 22–32 minutes per pass;
  - two passes gave identical results.
- **The Poison-Hunter sample largely agrees with it, within Tsuchiya's definition.**
  - Of the 105 Poison-Hunter rows that meet Tsuchiya's lookalike rule (at least 3 leading
    and 4 trailing hex characters), 89 (84.8%) match an archive transfer exactly: same
    transaction, victim and lookalike, and the same subtype every time.
  - The other 44 rows fall below that rule (mostly 2-character "dust"), so they cannot
    appear in the archive by construction. Still, for 37 of them the archive shows the same
    victim targeted by *other* lookalikes of the same address.
- **Payoffs link to earlier attempts.** 1,635 of the 1,738 confirmed payoffs (94.1%) have
  an archive poisoning transfer from the same lookalike to the same victim in an earlier
  block. The median gap is 19,953 blocks (about 2.8 days).
  - 62.6% of payoffs had a *rival* lookalike imitating the same intended address.
  - Among 1,007 linked payoffs from 852 victims, the paid lookalike was the most recent
    attempt 724 times (324.1 expected by chance) and the most similar 629 times (483.1
    expected). These are observational associations, not causal effects.
- **The Poison-Hunter outcomes stay as reported in Phase 11.2.** No row has an attempt-level
  payoff; one victim paid a competing lookalike (line 74); one lookalike had been paid
  before its planting (line 87).
- **No supervised benchmark is possible yet.**
  - Data integrity passes. Event identity, temporal integrity and leakage control are
    partial. Label validity, independent evaluation, baseline comparability and operational
    usefulness fail.
  - The archive's categories are the source's own heuristics: every poisoning row meets the
    source's 3+4 rule, so CryptoGuard's lookalike predicates agree with 100% of rows by
    construction.
  - No source contains verified negatives.
- **The useful product of this phase:**
  - a safe, tested ingestion path;
  - a reproducible reconciliation;
  - a local review queue of **1,469 payoff-linked attempt events**, candidates for
    independent `verified_attempt` labels.

## 2. Objectives

1. Verify the integrity of, and the parser for, the Tsuchiya payoff file and attempt
   archive without executing untrusted SQL or making RPC requests.
2. Reconcile three sources (Poison-Hunter sample, Tsuchiya attempts, Tsuchiya payoffs),
   with the four recorded incidents as separate case studies.
3. Keep attempt, victim, address and campaign outcomes apart; handle competing lookalikes
   and temporal order explicitly.
4. Decide, with evidence, whether these labels support a wallet-centric benchmark.

## 5. File integrity

- **At download:** byte count equal to the published size; MD5 equal to KiltHub's
  `computed_md5` and `supplied_md5`; SHA-256 recorded and pinned; the full gzip stream
  decompressed to nowhere to verify every CRC (9,850,567,622 bytes).
- **At every scan:** `scan-archive` re-verifies size, MD5 and SHA-256 against the pinned
  values before reading, and refuses a mismatch.
- **Resume.** The download went through in one transfer. Resume support is implemented and
  tested against a mock server: a `206` with a matching `Content-Range` is required, a
  `200` restarts from byte 0, and a completed file that fails verification is renamed
  `.corrupt`. The live server's range support was not exercised.

## 6. Actual archive format

- PostgreSQL 16.8 plain-format dump (pg_dump 16.9). The preamble contains `SET`
  statements, `SELECT pg_catalog.set_config(...)`, `CREATE DATABASE addrpoisoning`, the psql
  meta-command `\connect addrpoisoning`, one `CREATE TABLE public.address_poisoning_ethereum`
  with 18 columns, then one `COPY ... FROM stdin;` block of tab-separated rows ending with
  `\.`. The README's restore instruction pipes the dump into `psql`, which would execute
  `CREATE DATABASE` and `\connect`; CryptoGuard never does.
- Unit: **one row is one token transfer** (an ERC-20 `Transfer` log) with category flags:
  `intended_transfer`, `zero_value_transfer`, `tiny_transfer`, `counterfeit_token_transfer`,
  `payoff_transfer`, `payoff_transfer_unconfirmed`, `is_not_categorized`. A poisoning
  transfer is one flagged zero-value, tiny or counterfeit (the notebook's definition).
  Direction comes from `is_sender_victim`: the victim is the sender when true (a spoofed
  `transferFrom`-style planting), otherwise the receiver; the lookalike is the other side.
- Columns are mapped **by name from the COPY header**: the dump's order differs from the
  README's (`zero_value_transfer` precedes `tiny_transfer`).
- No log index, transaction index or timestamp: two identical transfers in one
  transaction cannot be told apart, and events in one block cannot be ordered.
- `value` and `value_usd` are PostgreSQL `real` (float4, about 7 significant digits):
  base-unit amounts above 2^24 are rounded, and `NaN` marks missing values.

## 7. Streaming parser (`ml/tsuchiya_archive.py`, `tsuchiya-archive/1`)

- **Never executes the dump.** Lines are matched against a whitelist of preamble prefixes
  (`--`, `SET`, `SELECT pg_catalog.set_config(`, `CREATE DATABASE`, `\connect`), one
  `CREATE TABLE` whose 18 column names and types must equal the README's, and one `COPY`
  whose column set must match. `INSERT`-format dumps, any other statement, another table, a
  second COPY block, a missing terminator and truncated gzip each raise a specific error;
  nothing partial is returned. A test confirms the reader opens no database at all.
- **Rows** are split on tabs (the COPY text format escapes tabs and newlines, so one
  physical line is one row; escapes are decoded) and type-checked: 32-byte hash,
  addresses, `t`/`f`/`\N` booleans, finite reals or `NaN`, integers. Bad rows are counted
  by reason with example line numbers; the read fails above 0.1% malformed. Lines longer
  than 4,096 bytes are rejected without being buffered whole.
- **Memory:** gzip is decompressed as a stream with bounded line reads. Exact distinct
  counts of millions of victims, lookalikes, transactions and transfer keys spill to 64
  bucket files per counter (`SpillDistinct`), so memory stays flat. Only rows whose sender,
  receiver or transaction is in a deterministic **target manifest** are stored, in SQLite
  (parameterised inserts, unique source line numbers, indexes on sender, receiver and
  transaction; a 5-million-row cap fails the scan rather than filling the disk).
- **Target manifest** (`data/ml/poisoning/archive_targets.json`, hashed): Poison-Hunter
  victims, lookalikes and planting transactions; payoff victims, lookalikes and
  transactions; incident victims, harm recipients and transactions. Imitated addresses are
  deliberately excluded: they can be exchange wallets with millions of rows, and every row
  involving a target victim already carries its intended address.
- **Recovery:** gzip has no record-level seek, so a resumed scan replays from byte 0. The
  database is built as `<name>.tmp` and renamed only after the whole pass; leftovers of an
  interrupted or killed run (temporary database, its SQLite rollback journal, spill files)
  are deleted first. A rerun with the same archive, manifest and parser is skipped as
  cached; a forced rerun produces identical content (tested).

## 8. Processing performance

| Run | Rows | Runtime | Throughput | Peak RSS | Temporary disk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full archive, first pass | 34,905,969 | 1,931 s | about 18,100 rows/s | 279 MiB | about 0.95 GiB (measured before the final flush) |
| Full archive, second pass (`--force`) | 34,905,969 | 1,296 s | about 26,900 rows/s | 287 MiB | 972 MiB |
| Synthetic, 1,000 rows | 1,000 | 3.0 s | 335 rows/s | 43 MiB | negligible |
| Synthetic, 10,000 rows | 10,000 | 4.1 s | 2,464 rows/s | 49 MiB | negligible |
| Synthetic, 100,000 rows | 100,000 | 8.4 s | 11,904 rows/s | 85 MiB | negligible |
| Reconciliation (301,370 stored rows) | - | 6–15 s | - | 339 MiB | - |

- **Small runs are dominated by fixed costs.** Opening files takes about 28 ms each on this
  machine; it was measured, probably because of antivirus scanning. The spill counter's
  256 bucket files cost about 14 s per run regardless of size.
- **Two performance defects were found and fixed.** A first version reopened the bucket
  files on every flush, which made the first million rows take 128 s. A first full attempt
  was stopped at 7 million rows and restarted with the fix.
- **Deterministic.** The two full passes gave identical summaries (all fields except
  timing and spill) and an identical stored subset (same SHA-256 over all 301,370 rows).
  The runtime difference (32 vs 22 minutes) came from other work on the machine during
  the first pass.
- **Memory stays flat.** Peak RSS grows with the in-memory deduplication buffers (500,000
  values per counter), not with archive size. The full pass stayed under the 512 MiB
  target.
- **Disk:**
  - archive 2.32 GB;
  - target subset 146 MB (SQLite);
  - temporary spill files of 972 MiB during the pass, deleted afterwards;
  - no decompressed copy is ever written.

## 10. Poison-Hunter overlap

Unit: Poison-Hunter row (149 valid rows: 143 distinct victim-events plus 6 duplicates).
All 149 rows fall inside the archive's poisoning-block range. Each row is assigned its
strongest match level.

| Level | Rows | Meets the 3+4 rule | Below it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `exact_tx_event` (same tx, victim, lookalike) | 89 | 89 (8 dust, 38 fake, 43 zero) | 0 |
| `tx_only` (same tx, other victim/lookalike) | 16 | 13 (12 fake, 1 zero) | 3 (zero) |
| `competing_lookalike` (victim listed with another lookalike of the same address) | 40 | 3 | 37 (dust) |
| `victim_only` | 3 | 0 | 3 |
| `none` | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| `victim_lookalike(_imitated)`, `shared_lookalike` | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **Agreement within the definition:** 89 of 105 rows (84.8%) match exactly, and the
  subtype agrees in all 89: dust→tiny, fake→counterfeit, zero→zero.
- **The 16 transaction-only rows are all batched plantings** (batches of 8–422 victims).
  - In 4 fake-token rows the archive lists the same victim in the same transaction but
    with a **different lookalike**: the sources disagree on the attacker address.
  - In 12 rows the victim is absent from that transaction in the archive. Three of these
    fall below 3+4. For the others the cause is not visible offline; one possibility is that
    Tsuchiya et al. require an observed intended transfer.
- **The 44 rows below 3+4 are a definition difference, not a disagreement about attacks.**
  The archive cannot contain them by construction.
- **"Tsuchiya-only" events are not measurable.** The Poison-Hunter file is a 150-row
  sample of an unpublished dataset, so most archive events are absent from it by design.

## 11. Payoff dataset overlap

- All 1,738 payoffs are in the archive. 1,634 carry the `payoff_transfer` flag. The other
  104 carry only `payoff_transfer_unconfirmed`; the authors confirmed those manually
  (notebook remark), which explains the gap.
- **Attempt linkage** (unit: payoff):
  - 1,635 (94.1%) have an earlier attempt from the same lookalike to the same victim:
    1,407 victims, 1,450 lookalikes.
  - 13 have attempts only after the payment.
  - 90 have none in the archive.
- **Timing and repetition.**
  - Gap from the last attempt to the payoff: p25 1,039 blocks (about 3.5 h), median 19,953
    (about 2.8 days), p75 82,432 (about 11 days), maximum 2,097,556 (about 290 days).
  - Most victims saw one attempt from the paid lookalike (median 1, p75 3, maximum 139).
- The 103 unlinked payoffs stay unlinked. They are not attributed to any other attempt.

## 12. Victim-level versus attempt-level outcomes

The four outcome levels (`ml/reconcile.py`) are computed separately for each Poison-Hunter
row:

| Outcome | Rows of 149 |
|---|---|
| Attempt: the victim paid **this** lookalike after the planting | 0 (0 within 7 days) |
| Victim: the victim paid **any** lookalike after the planting | 1 (line 74) |
| Address: this lookalike was paid by anyone **before** the planting | 1 (line 87) |
| Address: ... **after** the planting | 0 |
| Campaign: a lookalike in the row's campaign group was paid after the planting | 0 |

- A victim-level loss is never credited to an attempt.
- A payment before a planting is address history, never an outcome.
- Regression tests encode lines 74 and 87.

## 13. Competing lookalikes

- **Rivals are the norm.** Before their payoff, victims had been reached by a median of 3
  other lookalikes (p75 9, maximum 1,741). 1,088 payoffs (62.6%) had at least one rival
  imitating the *same* intended address.
- **Which rival got paid** (1,007 linked payoffs with such rivals, 852 victims):
  - the paid lookalike was the **most recent** attempt in 724 cases, against 324.1 expected
    if the victim had picked uniformly among the candidates;
  - it was the **most similar** (leading+trailing characters) in 629 cases, against 483.1
    expected; ties count, and the expectation accounts for them.
- **Interpretation.** Recency is the stronger association. It fits victims copying the
  latest entry in their history, but the data cannot show cause. Payoffs from one victim are
  not independent, and missing attempts (other tokens, ETH, outside the 3+4 rule) are
  unobserved. No confidence interval is reported because the payoffs are not a random
  sample of anything.
- **Poison-Hunter rows:** 40 rows show the victim targeted by other lookalikes of the same
  address.

## 14. Temporal correctness

- **Categories.** A (planting, payment) pair is `before_planting`, `same_block_order_unknown`
  (the archive has no transaction index), `after_planting` or `temporally_ambiguous` (a
  block is missing). Negative delays are never shown as lead times.
- **Payoff linkage** uses only attempts in strictly earlier blocks. Same-block attempts are
  reported separately: 0 payoffs were linked only by a same-block attempt.
- **No future-data leakage.** Payoff status is an evaluation outcome. It is never a feature,
  never in a review packet as a score, and never used to classify an earlier planting.
  The point-in-time event features (Phase 11.2) see only strictly earlier positions.
- **Not checked:** reorganisations. The archive gives no block hashes, so a reorg cannot be
  detected from it; its blocks are deep in history.

## 15. Dataset-selection bias

- **Lookalike rule:** the archive keeps only lookalikes with at least 3+4 matching
  characters, so poisoning with less similar addresses (41 of the Poison-Hunter dust rows
  use 2 leading characters) is invisible.
- **Tokens:**
  - payoffs are ERC-20 only, with no native ETH;
  - USD values exist only for priced tokens (counterfeit rows are NaN);
  - three of the four recorded incidents were paid in ETH or WBTC, or happened after
    June 2024.
- **Period:** July 2022 to June 2024 (blocks 15,053,226–20,208,032).
- **Detection pipeline:** what counts as a victim, an intended transfer or a tiny transfer
  (under 10 USD, per the notebook) is the source's definition.
- **Payoff confirmation:** 104 payoffs were promoted from "unconfirmed" by manual Etherscan
  review, so the payoff list mixes automated and manual evidence.

## 16. Detection-rule dependence

The agreement figures fall into five tiers of evidence; only the first was measured here.

| Agreement with | Result |
|---|---|
| The source's lookalike rule | 100% for CryptoGuard's detector predicate and candidate rule, **implied** by the 3+4 selection |
| The source's candidate detection (Poison-Hunter vs Tsuchiya) | 89/105 exact within the shared definition |
| Independently validated attacks | not measured: no independent attack labels exist |
| Independent victim outcomes | payoffs exist, but only for attempts the source already selected |
| Generalization to unselected events | not measurable from these sources |

CryptoGuard's single-transfer candidate rules on source-labelled rows:

- **The relevant rule cannot be checked here.** The source's poisoning is mostly spoofed
  *outgoing* transfers, which CryptoGuard's `spoofed_out` rule targets. That rule needs the
  planting transaction's signer, and the archive does not have it.
- **The rules that can be checked:**
  - `zero_value_in` fires on the 2.5% of zero-value rows the victim receives;
  - `dust_in` (at most 0.01 USDT/USDC) catches **9.3%** of the source's tiny transfers.
    This is a real threshold difference: the source's tiny goes up to 10 USD.
  - `lookalike_in` agrees with all incoming rows, by construction.

## 18. Candidate-generation evaluation

- Phase 11.2 reported candidate recall on the 4 recorded incidents (4 of 4, not untouched
  data).
- Phase 11.3 adds the rule coverage above, on 17.4 million source-labelled rows. It is a
  measure of agreement with the source's definitions, not detector recall:
  - the source's lookalike rule is a subset of CryptoGuard's;
  - the most common planting shape needs data (the signer) the archive lacks;
  - no ordinary wallet histories are available to measure false candidates.
- Wallet-level candidate generation needs full victim histories, which these sources do not
  contain.

## 19. Unknown and unmatched outcomes

- **The 97 pilot rows the Phase 11.2 pilot could not check.** The payoff list has no
  attempt-level payoff for any of them. That absence counts only for ERC-20 payments in the
  study period to lookalikes the study detected:
  - **62 of the 97 rows** (39 not evaluated, 23 unresolved) are exact archive matches, so
    for those the study demonstrably tracked the attempt. "No ERC-20 payoff to this
    lookalike was confirmed by July 2024" is supported for them.
  - The other 35 rows lack even that coverage condition.
  - It is not proof of no payment for any row (ETH, other tokens, after June 2024).
- **The 52 complete pilot rows:** 27 are exact matches, and both sources agree that no
  payoff occurred.
- **Unmatched records.**
  - 103 payoffs have no earlier attempt in the archive;
  - 12 batched Poison-Hunter rows are absent from their transaction in the archive;
  - 4 rows disagree on the lookalike;
  - 1 row has no archive trace at all.
  None of these is relabelled.

## 20. Statistical limitations

- **Denominators.** Every rate above names its unit and denominator.
- **No random samples.**
  - The Poison-Hunter file is a published sample of unknown design.
  - Payoffs are a selected subset of victims.
  - The pilot's complete rows were the least active victims.
  So no population rate, precision, recall or theft probability is reported.
- **Correlation.** Rows share victims, lookalikes, batch transactions and campaigns.
  Reported counts are descriptive; any later test needs grouping by victim and campaign.
- **Earlier conclusions stand.** The Phase 11.2 correction is unchanged: the pilot's 0.069
  bound applies only to the 52 completely checked rows.

## 23. ML readiness (decision gates)

| Gate | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Data integrity | **pass** | pinned hashes; 0 malformed rows; ten publisher counts reproduced |
| 2 Event identity | partial | attempts, transactions and victim-events are distinct, but the archive has no log index and no campaign identifier |
| 3 Label validity | **fail** | archive categories are the source's heuristics, not independent review of attempts |
| 4 Temporal integrity | partial | block-level order only; same-block pairs are "order unknown" |
| 5 Independent evaluation | **fail** | no verified negatives anywhere |
| 6 Leakage control | partial | grouping and grouped splits exist; there are no trainable labels to split |
| 7 Baseline comparability | **fail** | the archive lacks the wallet histories the detector needs |
| 8 Operational usefulness | **fail** | alert volume on ordinary wallets is known from 27 wallet-days only |

**Conclusion.** The available research data cannot support a valid supervised benchmark
for `poisoning-attempt/1`. No model was trained, and none should be until gates 3, 5 and 7
pass.

What the data does support:
- positive-case reproduction;
- agreement analysis between sources;
- retrospective payoff studies;
- a concrete review queue.

## 25. Recommended next experiment

matched legitimate look-alike interactions as negatives.**

  - by year;
  - by whether a rival lookalike existed;
  - with one packet per victim and campaign group.
  Reviewers confirm each planting on a block explorer, with no model score shown. **The
  sample of 60 is frozen (§26).**
- **Negatives.** For the same victims, take transfers from addresses that share 3+4 or more
  characters with a real counterparty but are legitimate, for example a counterparty's own
  second wallet. Finding and verifying them needs the victims' wallet histories.
  - This is the first step that would need new chain data, so it needs a separately
    approved, capped RPC budget.
  - It should be sized with `cryptoguard ml plan-collection` before any request is made.
- **Exact evidence needed:**
  - reviewer decisions in `poisoning-review/1` format;
  - for negatives, the victims' full transfer histories over the review window;
  - a frozen sampling manifest, written before any review.
- **What not to do:** no broad crawl, no paid service, no labels inferred from overlap.

## 26. Frozen review sample (2026-11-02, 0 RPC requests)

The positive half of §25 is ready for review. The sample was drawn once, before any review,
by `cryptoguard ml freeze-review-sample` (`ml/review_sample.py`, `review-sample/1`) at
commit `a273f8a` on a clean tree. Reviewer instructions and the analysis plan, fixed at the
same time: [docs/ml/review-protocol.md](docs/ml/review-protocol.md).

- **Design.**
  - **Campaign groups.** Packets that share a victim, lookalike, planting transaction or
    imitated address form one group: 1,469 packets in 1,395 groups, 62 of them with more
    than one packet (at most 6). Each group contributes one representative, so no two
    sampled packets share a victim or campaign.
  - **Strata.** Subtype × year (by block height) × whether a rival lookalike existed.
  - **Allocation.** Quotas per subtype: fake token 24, spoofed `transferFrom` 24, dust 9,
    zero-value 3 (all of them). Within a subtype, the quota is split in proportion to
    stratum size, with at least one per non-empty stratum.
  - **Selection.** A keyed SHA-256 ranking with a fixed seed, so it is reproducible by hand
    and independent of input order. The other 1,335 representatives are ordered reserves.
  - **Weights.** Each item's design weight is its stratum's group count divided by its
    selected count.
