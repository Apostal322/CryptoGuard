## 3. Ground truth

There is **no independent label** of which attempts share an operator: public reports describe single thefts, not attacker infrastructure. Pairwise precision/recall, ARI or NMI would have to score these rules against their own output, so none is reported. Incident manifests are used only to check, after the fact, where each theft's recipient ended up.

## 4. Relationship definitions

Planting-side entities only (never the targeted wallet): planting transaction (directly observed), lookalike and verified signer (strongly supported, merge), narrow token contract and deployer (tentative, context only), popular token contracts and block proximity (insufficient). A signer that planted more than 25 different lookalikes is a hub: reported, never merged. See `campaigns/relations.py` and DETECTION_METHODS.md.

## 8. Mathematical methods (ablations G and H)

- **G, diffusion**: Top-5 attempts outside each cluster ranked by heat diffusion (tau=1) on the planting-side graph, compared with hop distance, two-step shared-neighbour counts and personalized PageRank. Popular contracts and hub signers are removed first. Over 11 clusters, mean top-5 overlap of heat diffusion with hop distance 4.64, with personalized PageRank 5.0, with shared-neighbour counts 4.27 (of 5).
- **H, spectral**: Spectral gap = second-smallest normalized-Laplacian eigenvalue of each cluster's attempt-entity graph. A small gap indicates a weakly connected group. Articulation points identify such groups exactly and name the joining attempt or entity. 11 clusters, 3 chained; mean gap chained 0.0834 vs other 0.5459; the gap alone separates the two groups: yes.

## 11. Conclusions (computed from the tables above)

- **Cross-wallet links exist in real data.** With monitored wallets alone, 4 candidate clusters span more than one wallet; with the bounded expansion, 9 (largest: 31 attempts on 12 wallets). Multi-wallet clusters by single link type: shared signer 11, shared planting transaction 13, reused lookalike 1.
- **Context at the time of an attempt.** 23 of 54 attempts on monitored wallets used a planting signer already seen on another wallet earlier (point in time).
- **No earlier warning.** For 3 of 4 thefts the recipient's cluster spanned several wallets before the theft, but never earlier than the existing detector's own warning.
- **Guards matter.** Without them (popular contracts and every shared entity merging), the largest cluster grows from 31 to 58 attempts and from 12 to 34 wallets; block proximity alone would merge 95 otherwise unlinked pairs.
- **Diffusion adds no ranking information here**: its top-5 agrees with simpler methods on 4.27-5.0 of 5 attempts on average.
- **Spectral gap is redundant** with articulation points, which identify the same weakly connected (chained) clusters exactly and name the joining point.

## 12. Limitations

- No ground truth for common control; every cluster is a candidate grouping.
- Signer windows are about 150 blocks each side of one known attempt, capped at 6 transactions per signer: the signers seen here sign far more, so expansion is a small sample of their activity, not a census.
- Funding sources (incoming ETH) and factory-created contracts are invisible over standard JSON-RPC; a shared funder or deployer may exist without being seen.
- Background wallets were sampled excluding contracts and very busy wallets.
- Verification only checks ERC-20 payments within 100 blocks; targets whose real payment was native ETH or earlier remain unverified candidates.
